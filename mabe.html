Mabe Products
Usability Study

A moderated usability study of mabeproducts.com, co-conducted with Esha Perisetla. We tested how effectively the site supports parents shopping for luxury baby carriers and identified three actionable usability issues.

Overview

mabeproducts.com is an e-commerce platform selling luxury baby carriers. It relies on a minimalistic aesthetic with beige and pastel tones, high-quality lifestyle photography, and detailed product pages to guide customers from discovery to purchase. We selected it for usability testing because shopping for a baby carrier is a high-consideration decision: parents weigh safety, comfort, fit, cost, and style simultaneously, and the site needs to support all of those factors clearly and efficiently. Our evaluation was grounded in Nielsen Norman Group UX principles, applying Nielsen's (1994) ten usability heuristics as the analytical framework for identifying interface breakdowns and guiding our recommendations.

The Site

The homepage introduces users to the brand through featured products, empowering messaging, and calls to action. Navigation is structured at the top with a menu linking to product categories, brand information, and support resources. Individual product pages provide descriptions, images, and highlighted features intended to support the user's decision-making process.

Why We Tested It

The site depends on both visual presentation and detailed product information to ease what is ultimately a safety-critical purchase. When either element fails, users lose confidence and abandon the experience. This made it an effective platform for evaluating usability strengths and identifying specific gaps in information design and interface consistency.

Research Method

A moderated think-aloud approach using a mixed-method format: two sessions conducted in person on a laptop, and two conducted remotely via Zoom with screen sharing. Each participant followed the same structured protocol from start to finish.

1

Pre-Test Questionnaire

Gathered background on each participant's prior purchase experience, baby carrier usage, key decision factors, and concerns when shopping for baby products online.

2

Moderated Think-Aloud Sessions

Participants completed two task scenarios on the live site while verbalizing their thoughts, expectations, and moments of confusion in real time. Sessions ran approximately 20 minutes each.

3

Post-Test Questionnaire

Captured satisfaction, perceived ease of use, and specific pain points immediately after task completion, along with follow-up questions on what participants felt was missing or unclear.

4

Analysis and Recommendations

Observations and questionnaire data were synthesized to identify recurring patterns across participants, which were then mapped to three specific usability issues with targeted recommendations.

Findings and Recommendations

All four participants successfully completed both tasks, but consistent friction patterns emerged across sessions. Three usability issues were identified, each grounded in observed behavior and supported by established research.

Finding 1

Disappearing Product Options

Selecting certain color variants caused the entire color selection menu to disappear, creating a navigational dead-end. Two participants were forced to use the back button or restart the task. This violates the usability principles of visibility of system status and consistency: users should always be able to see available actions, and similar interface elements should behave predictably (Nielsen, 1994). When options disappear unexpectedly, users rely on memory rather than recognition, increasing cognitive load (Norman, 2013).

Recommendation

Keep the Color Menu Always Visible

Display the color selection menu on every product page regardless of stock status. If a color is unavailable, show it as greyed out or label it "Sold Out" rather than removing it. Consistent interface behavior prevents confusion and maintains user trust across the entire product browsing experience.

Finding 2

Small Font and Dense Text

Three of four participants reported difficulty reading product details due to small font sizes and large unbroken text blocks. Multiple participants zoomed in or lost focus mid-scan. Users do not read web content word for word; they scan for key information, and dense, small text actively disrupts that behavior (Krug, 2014). Poor typography reduces comprehension and task efficiency, particularly in contexts where fast decision-making is expected (Lidwell, Holden, & Butler, 2010).

Recommendation

Increase Readability Across Product Pages

Increase font size on product detail sections and restructure dense paragraphs into scannable bullet points. Apply clear typographic hierarchy with adequate spacing between sections so users can quickly identify and evaluate the information most relevant to their purchase decision.

Finding 3

Missing Age and Weight Information

Two participants could not determine the age range or weight capacity for the products from the product page. This safety-critical information existed only in the FAQ section, requiring users to navigate away at the exact moment they were evaluating a purchase. This breaks the usability principle of matching system organization to user expectations: information should be placed where users naturally look for it (Nielsen, 1994).

Recommendation

Surface Key Safety Details on the Product Page

Include age range and weight capacity directly in the product details section for every carrier. For parents evaluating safety and fit, this information is a primary decision factor. Requiring a secondary search for it introduces hesitation and erodes confidence at the most critical point in the purchase journey.

High-Fidelity Wireframes

Our recommendations were not just theoretical. We translated each finding into high-fidelity wireframes across four pages of the site, and Mabe Products implemented these changes on their live platform.
