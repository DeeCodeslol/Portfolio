The Challenge

As Campus Lead for Changemaker at ASU West Valley, I identified a core gap: the website was drawing in students but failing to turn their curiosity into action. Analytics from the 2024 to 2025 academic year made the problem impossible to ignore.

The issue wasn't a lack of content. Vague navigation labels, calls to action that bounced users to external forms with no context, and zero procedural guidance left students with more questions than answers before they ever found a reason to stay.

Research Process

Three structured evaluation methods assessed the site and built a research-backed case for change.

1

Heuristic Evaluation

Assessed the site against Nielsen's 10 usability principles to surface interface-level breakdowns in navigation, feedback, and consistency.

2

Content Audit

Catalogued every page, link, and call to action to map where information was missing, duplicated, or actively misleading users.

3

Comparative Review

Benchmarked the site against similar university program websites to identify patterns and opportunities the current design was missing.

Findings and Recommendations

Three core usability issues emerged from the evaluation, each paired with a targeted, research-backed solution.

Finding

Navigation Gaps

Application deadlines and program requirements were buried under inconsistent labels and deep page nesting. Users could not find what they needed without clicking through multiple dead ends.

Recommendation

Unified "Get Involved" Hub

Replace fragmented links with a single, clearly labeled section surfacing all participation pathways, roles, and deadlines in one place.

Finding

Transparency Issues

CTAs like "Join Changemaker" redirected users to external Google Forms with no response timeline or follow-up information, creating hesitation and discouraging completion.

Recommendation

Procedural Pages

Add step-by-step guides for each application and membership process, including expected response times and a point of contact for questions.

Finding

Content Inconsistency

Tone, terminology, and formatting varied across subpages and linked forms, creating a fragmented experience that reduced trust and made the site feel unreliable.

Recommendation

Content Style Guide

Standardize language, button labels, and layout hierarchy across all pages and forms to create a cohesive, trustworthy experience built for long-term maintenance.

Real-World Impact

This evaluation went beyond a classroom deliverable. Changemaker at ASU West Valley reviewed the findings and acted on the recommendations, applying the proposed changes directly to their live website.

✓
Recommendations Implemented by Changemaker at ASU West Valley

The organization took the research seriously. The navigation restructuring, procedural transparency improvements, and content consistency updates were all incorporated into the live site, turning a student-led evaluation into measurable organizational change.
